## 6. Bought / resold Claude account (2.0/10)

A bought account is the weakest pick in this set. Anthropic's terms are written for individual, non-transferable accounts. Sharing, selling, or handing over an account falls outside what the terms permit, and a subscription bought through a reseller does not change that. There is no version of the purchase that makes it compliant.

A password is not ownership. The seller typically keeps the recovery email, the phone number used at sign-up, and the payment method on file. Any one of those is a master key that outranks the password the buyer received. If a dispute ever reaches support, the creator can prove the account is theirs. The buyer can prove only that they once received a password from a stranger.

From Anthropic's side, a sold account behaves exactly like a stolen one. The login location jumps to a new country, the device changes, and usage patterns shift at once. Automated security systems respond to that pattern with challenges, verification requests, temporary locks, and reviews. Accounts with abrupt identity changes are the first to be restricted or closed when checks tighten.

No buyer can audit what the account did before the sale. Strikes and trust scores attach to the account, not to the person. The same seller can sell the same account again and again, because the recovery options stay with them. Bulk accounts share fingerprints — the same seller, the same creation batch, similar recovery details — so when one is identified, the rest fall together.

Payments run on methods chosen for the seller's protection: cryptocurrency, gift cards, and peer-to-peer transfers that cannot be reversed. Once the money moves, it is gone, whatever happens to the account afterwards.

Pros: instant access; no sign-up step.

Cons: terms violation from day one; original owner keeps the keys; your prompts, files, and projects sit in someone else's account; sudden bans; no refund path; the buyer inherits an enforcement profile they cannot see and cannot repair.

## 8. Pitfalls

Do not build inside an account a stranger can reclaim. Do not paste client material — contracts, strategy, unreleased code, personal data — into an account whose recovery path you do not control. If the seller recovers it, client information has effectively been disclosed to an unauthorised third party.

Do not trust warm-up routines. Log in gently, chat casually for a week, change details slowly — none of it changes the recovery email, the creation identity, or the terms violation underneath. At best, warm-up delays detection. At worst, it deepens your investment before the inevitable loss.

Do not trust "aged" as a safety signal. Most aged accounts on the resale market were farmed: created in batches, kept alive with scripted or minimal activity, and warehoused until a buyer appeared. Age proves a creation date and nothing else.

If you already bought one, treat migration as urgent maintenance. Export and copy out everything that matters while access lasts. Then open your own account, move the work across, and rebuild your history there. Stop adding new material to the bought account, and never place client data or payment details inside it again.
